# PART 1 — REASONING ARCHITECTURE

---

# 1. Purpose

The AI Reasoning Engine transforms validated research and evidence into structured conclusions, investment judgments, and explainable decisions.

Its responsibility is reasoning only.

It never performs planning, execution orchestration, module selection, framework selection, research collection, auditing, or output generation.

The Reasoning Engine receives validated ResearchObject and EvidenceObject inputs and produces a standardized DecisionObject for downstream quality validation.

---

# 2. Vision

Provide institutional-grade reasoning that is:

- Logical  
- Evidence-driven  
- Transparent  
- Consistent  
- Probabilistic  
- Explainable  
- Decision-oriented  
- Reproducible  

Every conclusion should remain traceable to supporting evidence, explicit assumptions, and a visible reasoning trail.

---

# 3. Design Philosophy

The Reasoning Engine follows seven principles.

## Principle 1 — Reason after research

Reasoning begins only after sufficient validated evidence has been collected.

## Principle 2 — Evidence before conclusions

Conclusions must emerge from evidence rather than from narrative or expectation.

## Principle 3 — Multiple reasoning methods

No single method is universally optimal. Different problems require different reasoning approaches.

## Principle 4 — Probabilistic thinking

Avoid absolute certainty. Express conclusions with confidence, uncertainty, and sensitivity.

## Principle 5 — Explainability

Every conclusion must explain why it was reached, how it was reached, what evidence supported it, and what could invalidate it.

## Principle 6 — Independent reasoning

Reasoning remains independent from planning, execution, research collection, and quality auditing.

## Principle 7 — Institutional consistency

Reasoning should resemble the analytical process of an institutional investment committee rather than a retail opinion engine.

---

# 4. Responsibilities

The Reasoning Engine shall:

- Interpret validated research and evidence.
- Construct logical arguments.
- Generate hypotheses.
- Evaluate competing hypotheses.
- Identify causal relationships.
- Estimate probabilities.
- Analyze trade-offs.
- Analyze scenarios.
- Estimate confidence.
- Form conviction.
- Generate DecisionObject.
- Provide explainable reasoning trails.

---

# 5. Non-Responsibilities

The Reasoning Engine shall never:

- Interpret user intent.
- Generate execution plans.
- Select modules.
- Select frameworks.
- Query registries.
- Manage runtime state.
- Allocate context.
- Collect research.
- Validate research quality.
- Generate reports or presentation formats.
- Perform output formatting.
- Audit outputs.

These responsibilities belong to other core engines.

Every reasoning process must be:

# PART 2 — CORE REASONING METHODOLOGIES

---

# 9. Deductive Reasoning

Purpose

Derive conclusions that logically follow from established facts.

Use Cases

- Rule-based validation
- Accounting consistency
- Financial statement relationships
- Regulatory interpretation

Output

Logical conclusions with deterministic confidence where facts fully determine the result.

---

# 10. Inductive Reasoning

Purpose

Generalize from observed evidence.

Use Cases

- Pattern recognition
- Historical trend analysis
- Business quality assessment
- Industry comparisons

Output

Probabilistic conclusions based on observed patterns.

---

# 11. Abductive Reasoning

Purpose

Identify the most plausible explanation for incomplete evidence.

Use Cases

- Turnaround analysis
- Management actions
- Unexpected financial changes
- Strategic shifts
- Macro interpretation

Output

Ranked hypotheses with supporting evidence.

---

# 12. Bayesian Reasoning

Purpose

Continuously update probabilities as new evidence becomes available.

Applications

- Investment thesis evolution
- Catalyst monitoring
- Quarterly results
- Policy changes
- Risk reassessment

Every significant evidence update should modify confidence rather than replacing prior reasoning entirely.

---

# 13. Causal Reasoning

Purpose

Distinguish causation from correlation.

Validate:

- Cause
- Effect
- Intermediate mechanisms
- External influences
- Feedback loops

Applications

- Revenue growth
- Margin expansion
- Capital allocation
- Industry cycles
- Competitive advantage
- Macroeconomic impacts

---

# 14. First-Principles Reasoning

Purpose

Reduce complex problems to fundamental truths before rebuilding conclusions.

Applications

- Business model analysis
- Economic moat evaluation
- Capital allocation
- Industry disruption
- Technology adoption
- Valuation assumptions

The engine must avoid relying solely on historical precedent when first-principles analysis is more appropriate.

---

# 15. Systems Thinking

Purpose

Analyze companies as interconnected systems rather than isolated metrics.

Consider interactions between:

- Management
- Capital allocation
- Customers
- Suppliers
- Competitors
- Regulation
- Technology
- Macroeconomics
- Cash flow
- Growth
- Governance

Systems thinking emphasizes relationships, feedback loops, and long-term dynamics.

---

# 16. Analogical Reasoning

Purpose

Use relevant historical analogies to improve understanding while avoiding false equivalence.

Applications

- Business transformations
- Industry evolution
- Technology adoption
- Capital cycle analysis
- Turnaround comparisons
- Market structure changes

Analogies are supporting evidence, never primary evidence.

---

Complex investment decisions often require multiple reasoning methodologies simultaneously.

Example flow:

The Reasoning Engine dynamically combines methods according to the complexity and nature of the problem.

---

# 19. Evidence Weighting

Not all evidence has equal importance.

Each evidence item receives a weight based on:

- Reliability
- Materiality
- Recency
- Independence
- Consistency
- Predictive Value

Evidence categories:

- Critical
- Major
- Supporting
- Minor
- Weak

Evidence weighting determines reasoning influence, not final confidence by itself.

## Evidence Anchoring Rule (Addition)

A conclusion's primary support must come from Critical or Major-tier
evidence (per the categories above). Supporting or Minor-tier evidence
may reinforce a conclusion but shall never independently justify it.
If only Weak-tier evidence exists for a material claim, the claim must
be labeled "insufficiently supported" rather than included in the
Investment Thesis at full confidence.

---

The Reasoning Engine shall evaluate multiple explanations simultaneously.

Required hypotheses include:

- Primary Hypothesis

- Contradictory Hypothesis
- Null Hypothesis

Unsupported hypotheses are discarded through evidence comparison.

# 27. Contradiction Detection

## Detection Method (Addition — run before the verification checklist below)

For every pair of evidence items addressing the same claim, check:
- Do they state opposite directional facts?
- Could a difference in time period explain the apparent conflict,
  rather than a genuine contradiction?
- Is one item materially more current than the other?

Only a difference that survives this check is a genuine contradiction
requiring the verification checklist below.

## Verification Checklist

Before approving any decision, verify:

- Conclusions align with evidence.
- No conflicting assumptions remain unresolved.
- Financial analysis supports valuation.
- Risk assessment aligns with recommendation.
- Alternative hypotheses were considered.

Material contradictions reduce confidence and require further review.

## High-Reliability Reasoning Addendum

For every material conclusion, create a compact **hypothesis ledger** before deciding: (1) decision and horizon, (2) base case, (3) strongest alternative/counter-case, (4) evidence for and against each, (5) key assumption, (6) falsifier, and (7) confidence impact. Do not let the amount of supporting prose substitute for comparison with the strongest credible alternative.

Use this sequence: frame → identify base rates and causal drivers → collect dated evidence → test alternative explanations → separate correlation from causal claim → identify disconfirming evidence → apply risk gates → state a conditional conclusion. When evidence is mixed, report what would resolve the disagreement instead of forcing a winner.
