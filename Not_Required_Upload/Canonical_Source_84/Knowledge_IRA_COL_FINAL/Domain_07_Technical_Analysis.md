# Domain 7 — Technical Analysis
Version: 1.0 | Status: Production Ready

---

## Purpose
Technical analysis studies price and volume behavior to assess market sentiment and timing. It is a complementary tool to fundamental analysis, not a substitute for business quality assessment.

---

## Core Concepts

### 1. Trend
- Definition: General direction of price movement over time (uptrend, downtrend, sideways/range-bound).
- Identification: Sequence of higher highs/higher lows (uptrend) or lower highs/lower lows (downtrend).
- Use: Establishes the dominant market bias before applying other tools.

### 2. Support
- Definition: Price level where buying interest has historically been strong enough to halt or reverse a decline.
- Use: Potential entry zones or downside risk reference points.

### 3. Resistance
- Definition: Price level where selling interest has historically capped upward movement.
- Use: Potential exit zones or upside target reference points.

### 4. Volume
- Definition: Number of shares/contracts traded over a period.
- Use: Confirms conviction behind a price move — rising price on rising volume signals stronger trend validity than a move on thin volume.

### 5. Momentum
- Definition: Rate of change of price movement.
- Indicators: RSI (Relative Strength Index), MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence), Rate of Change (ROC).
- Use: Identifies overbought/oversold conditions and potential trend exhaustion.

### 6. Relative Strength
- Definition: Performance of a stock/sector relative to a benchmark index or peer group.
- Use: Identifies leadership and laggard stocks within the market for tactical positioning.

### 7. Moving Averages
- Types: Simple Moving Average (SMA), Exponential Moving Average (EMA).
- Common periods: 20-day, 50-day, 100-day, 200-day.
- Use: Smooths price data to identify trend direction; crossovers (e.g., 50-day crossing above 200-day, "Golden Cross") are widely tracked signals.

### 8. Breakouts
- Definition: Price moving decisively beyond a defined support/resistance level or consolidation range, typically on higher volume.
- Use: Signals potential start of a new directional move; false breakouts are common and require volume/follow-through confirmation.

### 9. Volatility
- Definition: Degree of price fluctuation over a given period.
- Indicators: Average True Range (ATR), Bollinger Bands, India VIX (market-wide volatility gauge).
- Use: Informs position sizing and risk management, not just direction.

### 10. Market Structure
- Definition: The overall pattern of price action — accumulation, markup, distribution, markdown phases (Wyckoff-style framework).
- Use: Provides context for where the current trend sits within a larger cyclical pattern.

---

## Application Framework for Research
1. Use technical analysis to time entries/exits and manage risk — never to override fundamental business quality conclusions.
2. Combine trend + volume + momentum for higher-conviction signals rather than relying on a single indicator.
3. Always define invalidation levels (stop-loss/support break) before acting on a technical setup.

---

## Red Flags / Cautions
- Chasing breakouts without volume confirmation.
- Using technical signals alone to justify long-term investment in a fundamentally weak business.
- Over-optimizing indicator parameters to fit historical price action (curve-fitting).
- Ignoring broader market/index trend context when analyzing an individual stock.

---

## Universal Rule Applied to This Domain
Technical analysis improves timing, not business quality — this principle governs how it is combined with the rest of the Knowledge Library. It should never be presented as a standalone justification for fundamental investment merit.

---

## Worked Example
Stock breaks above a ₹500 resistance level (tested 3 times over 6 months) on volume 2.5x its 20-day average — a high-conviction breakout signal. Same ₹500 break on volume just 0.8x average, a week later, retraces within 3 days — a false breakout. The distinguishing variable was volume confirmation, not the price level itself.
