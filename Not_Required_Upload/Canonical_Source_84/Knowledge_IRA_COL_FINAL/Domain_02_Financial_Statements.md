# Domain 2 — Financial Statements
Version: 1.0 | Status: Production Ready

---

## Purpose
Financial statements are the primary factual record of a company's economic activity. This domain defines how to read, cross-reference, and extract insight from each statement type.

---

## Core Statements

### 1. Income Statement (Profit & Loss Statement)
- Purpose: Shows revenue, costs, and profit over a period (quarter/year).
- Key lines: Revenue from Operations, Other Income, Total Expenses, EBITDA, Depreciation & Amortization, EBIT, Finance Costs, Profit Before Tax (PBT), Tax Expense, Profit After Tax (PAT/Net Profit), EPS.
- Research use: Track revenue growth quality (volume vs. price led), margin trends, and one-off/exceptional items separately from core operations.

### 2. Balance Sheet
- Purpose: Snapshot of assets, liabilities, and equity at a point in time.
- Structure: Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders' Equity.
- Key sections:
  - Non-Current Assets: Property, Plant & Equipment (PP&E), Intangibles, Investments, Goodwill.
  - Current Assets: Inventory, Trade Receivables, Cash & Equivalents.
  - Equity: Share Capital, Reserves & Surplus.
  - Non-Current Liabilities: Long-term Borrowings, Deferred Tax Liabilities.
  - Current Liabilities: Trade Payables, Short-term Borrowings, Provisions.
- Research use: Assess capital structure, working capital intensity, and asset quality.

### 3. Cash Flow Statement
- Purpose: Tracks actual cash movement, distinct from accrual-based profit.
- Three sections:
  - Cash Flow from Operations (CFO): Cash generated from core business.
  - Cash Flow from Investing (CFI): Capex, asset sales, investment purchases/sales.
  - Cash Flow from Financing (CFF): Debt raised/repaid, dividends paid, equity issued/bought back.
- Research use: CFO vs. PAT comparison reveals earnings quality — persistent divergence is a red flag.
- Free Cash Flow (FCF) = CFO – Capex; a critical valuation and financial health input.

### 4. Notes to Accounts
- Purpose: Detailed disclosures explaining line items in the three main statements.
- Contains: Accounting policy details, contingent liabilities, related party transactions, segment-wise reporting, breakup of "other expenses," employee benefit details.
- Research use: Often where the real analytical insight and red flags are found — never skip notes.

### 5. Annual Reports
- Purpose: Comprehensive yearly disclosure combining financials, management commentary, and governance details.
- Key sections: Directors' Report, Management Discussion & Analysis (MD&A), Corporate Governance Report, Auditor's Report, Business Responsibility & Sustainability Report (BRSR).
- Research use: MD&A provides management's own narrative on performance and outlook — cross-check against actual numbers for consistency.

### 6. Quarterly Reports
- Purpose: Interim unaudited or limited-review financial disclosures (Q1-Q4).
- Use: Track trend continuity, seasonality effects, and near-term momentum.
- Caution: Quarterly numbers can be more volatile and less audited than annual figures — treat with proportionate scrutiny.

### 7. Consolidated Statements
- Purpose: Combines financials of parent company with all subsidiaries, associates, and joint ventures.
- Use: Reflects the true economic entity, including minority interests.
- Research use: Preferred for holistic group-level analysis, especially for companies with significant subsidiary operations.

### 8. Standalone Statements
- Purpose: Financials of the parent entity alone, excluding subsidiaries.
- Use: Useful for understanding the core listed entity's performance in isolation, especially for dividend capacity and lender covenants specific to the parent.
- Research use: Compare standalone vs. consolidated to identify subsidiary drag or contribution.

---

## Cross-Statement Analysis Framework
1. Always read all three statements together — never analyze P&L in isolation.
2. Reconcile PAT (P&L) with CFO (Cash Flow) — divergence signals need for investigation.
3. Trace balance sheet changes (e.g., rising receivables) back to underlying business reasons in notes.
4. Compare standalone vs. consolidated to understand subsidiary/JV impact.

---

## Red Flags / Cautions
- Profit growth not backed by cash flow growth over multiple periods.
- Frequent "exceptional items" normalizing otherwise poor core performance.
- Related party transactions embedded in notes without adequate MD&A explanation.
- Restated prior-year figures without clear disclosure of reasons.
- Auditor qualifications or emphasis-of-matter paragraphs.

---

## Universal Rule Applied to This Domain
Never invent or estimate financial figures. Extract all numbers directly from filed statements and disclose clearly if a figure is unavailable or unaudited.

---

## Worked Example
Company reports PAT growth of 20% (₹100cr→₹120cr) but CFO fell from ₹95cr to ₹60cr in the same year. Trace it: Notes show trade receivables jumped ₹45cr — revenue was recognized but cash not collected. Conclusion: reported profit growth is not yet cash-backed; flag as a quality concern pending next-quarter collection trend, not an automatic red flag.
