# Domain 18 — ESG & Sustainability
Version: 1.0 | Status: Production Ready

## Purpose
Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) factors are increasingly material to long-term risk assessment and institutional capital allocation decisions. This domain provides a structured framework for evaluating non-financial risk.

## Core Concepts

**1. Environmental**
- Carbon emissions (Scope 1, 2, 3) and decarbonization targets/glide path.
- Resource intensity: water usage, waste management, energy efficiency.
- Regulatory exposure: environmental clearances, pollution control compliance, carbon tax/border adjustment risk (e.g., EU CBAM for exporters).
- Physical climate risk: exposure to extreme weather, water stress in operating regions.

**2. Social**
- Employee metrics: attrition, diversity, health & safety record, unionization/labor relations.
- Supply chain: labor practices among suppliers, human rights due diligence.
- Community impact: land acquisition disputes, local community relations, CSR spend effectiveness (mandatory 2% net profit in India under Companies Act).
- Product responsibility: safety recalls, data privacy practices, customer grievance redressal.

**3. Governance** (cross-reference Domain 8 for full framework)
- Board independence and diversity, executive compensation alignment with performance, audit quality, related party transaction discipline.

**4. BRSR (Business Responsibility & Sustainability Report)**
- Mandatory SEBI disclosure framework for top listed Indian companies (by market cap), covering ESG metrics in a standardized format.
- Research use: Primary structured source for India-listed company ESG data — prefer over third-party ESG scores where BRSR data is available.

**5. ESG Rating Agencies**
- Examples: MSCI ESG, Sustainalytics, CRISIL ESG, domestic rating agencies.
- Caution: Ratings methodologies differ significantly across providers; a company can receive divergent scores from different agencies — never treat a single rating as definitive.

**6. Materiality Assessment**
- Definition: Identifying which ESG factors are financially material to a specific industry (e.g., carbon intensity is highly material for cement/steel, less so for IT services).
- Use: Apply industry-specific materiality maps (e.g., SASB standards) rather than generic ESG checklists.

## Application Framework
1. Identify industry-specific material ESG factors before conducting assessment (avoid generic, low-relevance scoring).
2. Prioritize primary disclosures (BRSR, annual report sustainability sections) over aggregated third-party scores.
3. Treat ESG as a risk-identification lens feeding into overall risk assessment (Domain 9), not a standalone investment screen unless explicitly requested.

## Red Flags / Cautions
- "Greenwashing" — sustainability claims in marketing/investor materials not matched by verifiable disclosed data or targets.
- Significant gap between stated ESG commitments and actual capex/spending allocated toward them.
- Pending environmental litigation or regulatory action not prominently disclosed.
- Governance red flags (Domain 8) coexisting with strong environmental/social scores — governance failures often undermine broader ESG credibility.

## Universal Rule Applied
ESG assessments must be evidence-based and sourced from disclosed data (BRSR, annual reports, verified third-party ratings) — never inferred from marketing language or assumed from industry stereotypes.

---

## Worked Example
A cement company's sustainability report highlights "40% renewable energy target by 2030" prominently, but BRSR disclosure shows current renewable mix at just 3%, with no disclosed capex allocation toward the transition. Target exists on paper without a funded roadmap — a greenwashing flag, not a validated ESG strength.
