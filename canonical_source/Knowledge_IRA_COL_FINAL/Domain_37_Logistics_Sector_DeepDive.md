# Domain 37 — Logistics Sector Deep Dive
Version: 1.0 | Status: Production Ready (New Domain)

## Purpose
Adds sub-segment structure and stock-picking context for logistics beyond Domain 12's one-line coverage (fleet utilization, freight rates, warehousing utilization).

## Sub-Segment Structure

**1. Road Freight (Full Truckload/Part Truckload)** — Trucking-based transport. Characteristics: highly fragmented industry with low barriers to entry at small scale, asset-owning players face fuel-cost and driver-availability risk, asset-light aggregator models (tech-enabled freight matching) are a distinct, less capital-intensive sub-model.

**2. Warehousing & Contract Logistics** — Managed warehousing, fulfillment services for corporates/e-commerce. Characteristics: GST implementation drove structural shift from fragmented state-wise warehousing to consolidated large-format warehouses — a multi-year structural tailwind; long-term contracts with corporates provide revenue visibility similar to REIT-style lease economics.

**3. Cold Chain Logistics** — Temperature-controlled logistics for pharma/perishables. Characteristics: higher capital intensity and specialization, higher margins than generic logistics, structural growth from pharma/food processing formalization (cross-reference Domain 12 Agriculture/Pharma).

**4. Express/Parcel Delivery & Last-Mile** — E-commerce-driven parcel delivery. Characteristics: volume-driven, margin sensitive to delivery density (higher density = better unit economics), intensely competitive with e-commerce platforms sometimes building in-house capability (disintermediation risk for pure third-party players).

**5. Rail & Multimodal Logistics** — Container rail freight, multimodal transport including rail+road combinations. Characteristics: benefits from Dedicated Freight Corridor (DFC) infrastructure completion (structural tailwind — cross-reference Domain 39 Railways), generally lower cost per tonne-km than road for long-haul, but less flexible for last-mile.

**6. Ports & Shipping-Linked Logistics** — Port logistics, container freight stations, shipping-adjacent services. Characteristics: tied to trade volume cycles (Domain 1 Global Trade), benefits from India's port capacity expansion and trade growth.

## Structural Themes Specific to This Sector
- **GST-driven warehouse consolidation**: post-GST, companies increasingly consolidate warehousing into fewer, larger, more efficient hubs rather than state-wise fragmented storage — track large-format warehousing capacity growth as a structural indicator.
- **E-commerce logistics demand**: structural growth driver, but increasingly captured in-house by large e-commerce platforms — assess whether a third-party logistics player has differentiated capability (cold chain, specialized handling, geographic reach) that resists disintermediation.
- **DFC (Dedicated Freight Corridor) commissioning**: progressive commissioning shifts long-haul freight economics toward rail — logistics companies with rail-linked capability stand to benefit disproportionately as sections complete.
- **Fuel cost pass-through**: assess whether contracts have fuel cost escalation clauses (structurally lower-risk) vs. fixed-rate contracts absorbing fuel volatility into margin.

## Stock-Picking Specific Angles (Small/Mid-Cap Logistics Focus)
1. **Asset-light vs. asset-heavy model distinction**: asset-light aggregators show better ROCE/scalability but more competitive intensity; asset-heavy players have higher entry barriers but higher capital intensity and cyclical earnings — match the model to the specific investment thesis (growth/scalability vs. steady cash generation).
2. **Warehousing occupancy and contract tenure**: similar logic to Domain 35's REIT framework — long-tenure corporate contracts provide revenue visibility; track occupancy trend and any large client concentration.
3. **DFC-linked rail logistics beneficiaries**: companies with rail-siding access or dedicated rail logistics capability positioned to capture volume shift as DFC sections commission — verify actual operational readiness, not just thematic exposure.
4. **Cold chain specialization**: niche players serving pharma/high-value perishables command better pricing and stickier contracts than generic cold storage — differentiate specialized vs. commodity cold chain exposure.

## Worked Example
A warehousing logistics company shows large-format warehouse capacity growing from 2 million sq ft to 3.5 million sq ft over 2 years, with average contract tenure with corporate clients at 5+ years and top-5 client concentration falling from 55% to 40% of revenue as new clients are added. This combination — structural capacity growth, long contract visibility, and improving client diversification — is a stronger thesis than warehousing exposure alone without checking contract tenure/concentration.

## Red Flags / Cautions
- Assuming e-commerce logistics exposure is automatically a durable tailwind without checking disintermediation risk from platforms building in-house capability.
- Ignoring fuel cost pass-through terms in freight/logistics contracts — fixed-rate contracts in a rising fuel cost environment compress margins materially.
- Treating "asset-light" as automatically superior without recognizing the correspondingly lower barriers to entry and higher competitive intensity in that model.

## Conflict Rule
