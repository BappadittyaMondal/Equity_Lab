# Domain 17 — Research Sources
Version: 1.0 | Status: Production Ready

## Purpose
Define the hierarchy and characteristics of acceptable research sources to ensure all conclusions are grounded in verifiable, primary evidence.

## Preferred Sources

**1. Annual Reports** — Comprehensive audited yearly disclosure; highest reliability for full-year financial and governance data.

**2. Quarterly Reports** — Interim disclosures (unaudited/limited review); useful for trend tracking, lower reliability than annual audited figures.

**3. Investor Presentations** — Company-prepared summaries accompanying results; useful for management's framing but should be cross-checked against actual filed financials.

**4. Conference Calls (Earnings Call Transcripts)** — Management commentary and analyst Q&A; valuable for forward-looking color and management tone, but represents opinion/guidance, not audited fact.

**5. SEBI Filings** — Regulatory disclosures (shareholding patterns, insider trading disclosures, takeover/SAST filings); authoritative for ownership and compliance data.

**6. Exchange Filings (NSE/BSE)** — Corporate announcements, material event disclosures; primary source for corporate actions and material developments.

**7. Government Data** — Official statistics (MoSPI, RBI, Ministry of Finance, Census); authoritative for macroeconomic indicators.

**8. Industry Reports** — Sector research from credible industry bodies/associations (CRISIL, ICRA, industry federations); useful for sector-wide benchmarking, verify publisher credibility.

**9. Official Company Websites** — Primary source for policies, disclosures, historical filings archive.

**10. Trusted Financial Databases** — Established data aggregators (exchange-provided data, recognized financial data providers); useful for cross-verification and historical series, but always reconcile against primary filings for critical figures.

## Additional Sources (Expansion)

**11. MCA Filings (Ministry of Corporate Affairs)** — Corporate filings including director details, charges/pledges, financial statements of unlisted subsidiaries — critical for group structure and related-party analysis.

**12. RBI Data & Publications** — Monetary policy reports, Financial Stability Reports, sectoral credit data — authoritative for banking/NBFC sector and macro-financial analysis.

**13. CMIE (Centre for Monitoring Indian Economy)** — Independent economic database covering corporate financials, unemployment data, capex tracking — useful cross-verification source for macro and corporate trend data.

## Source Reliability Hierarchy (applies across all domains)
1. Live Verified Data
2. Regulatory Filings (SEBI, MCA, Exchange)
3. Company Disclosures (Annual/Quarterly Reports)
4. Financial Statements (audited)
5. Official Government Sources (RBI, MoSPI)
6. Established Financial Knowledge/Databases
7. Academic Research
8. Historical Market Knowledge

## Application Framework
1. Always prefer primary filed documents over secondary summaries or media reports.
2. Cross-verify any single-source claim (especially from investor presentations or conference calls) against audited filings where material to conclusions.
3. Explicitly cite the source type used for each key data point in research output.

## Red Flags / Cautions
- Relying on media articles or social media as primary evidence for financial claims.
- Treating management commentary (calls/presentations) as equivalent in reliability to audited statements.
- Using outdated database figures without checking against the latest filed data.

## Universal Rule Applied
Never rely primarily on rumours or unverified social media. Every material claim must be traceable to a source within this hierarchy, with reliability level disclosed where relevant.

---

## Worked Example
An investor presentation claims "industry-leading margins." Cross-check: audited annual report shows EBITDA margin 18% vs. two listed peers at 22% and 24% — the claim doesn't hold under primary-source verification. Presentation language should never be accepted without reconciliation against filed financials.

## AI Trigger Keywords
where did this data come from, source, is this verified, latest annual report, official filing.

## Cross-Domain Links

## Conflict Rule
When sources conflict, follow the Source Reliability Hierarchy strictly — never average a primary filing against a secondary summary.
