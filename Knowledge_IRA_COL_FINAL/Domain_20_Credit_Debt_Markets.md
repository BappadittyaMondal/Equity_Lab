# Domain 20 — Credit & Debt Markets
Version: 1.0 | Status: Production Ready

## Purpose
Debt market analysis provides an independent, often earlier signal of financial stress or strength than equity markets, and is essential for evaluating leveraged companies and fixed-income context.

## Core Concepts

**1. Credit Ratings**
- Providers (India): CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, India Ratings; global: S&P, Moody's, Fitch.
- Scale (long-term, illustrative): AAA (highest safety) down through D (default).
- Notches: Outlook (Positive/Stable/Negative) signals rating trajectory before an actual rating action.
- Research use: Rating downgrades (or negative outlook) are important corroborating signals for financial risk deterioration flagged in equity analysis.

**2. Corporate Bonds / NCDs (Non-Convertible Debentures)**
- Definition: Debt instruments issued by companies to raise funds, not convertible to equity.
- Key terms: Coupon rate, tenure, secured/unsecured, credit rating, covenant structure.
- Research use: Spread over comparable G-Sec yield reflects market-assessed credit risk premium.

**3. Credit Spread**
- Definition: Yield difference between a corporate bond and a risk-free government security of similar tenure.
- Use: Widening spreads signal rising perceived credit risk (company-specific or sector-wide); useful leading indicator alongside equity research.

**4. Bank Loan vs. Bond Financing**
- Bank loans: Typically more flexible, covenant-heavy, relationship-based; may include working capital facilities.
- Bonds: Public/private placement, subject to rating agency scrutiny, wider investor base.
- Research use: Financing mix and diversification of funding sources indicates financial flexibility.

**5. Debt Covenants**
- Types: Financial covenants (leverage ratio caps, DSCR minimums), negative covenants (restrictions on further borrowing/asset sales), affirmative covenants (reporting requirements).
- Research use: Covenant headroom (distance to breach) is a critical but often under-analyzed risk indicator — breach can trigger technical default even if the company is operationally sound.

**6. Default & Restructuring**
- Definitions: Default (failure to meet payment obligation), Restructuring (renegotiation of debt terms — tenure extension, interest reduction, haircut).
- India-specific: Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) process, National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) resolution mechanism.
- Research use: Track any restructuring history or IBC proceedings as significant governance/financial red flags.

**7. Yield Curve**
- Definition: Plot of bond yields across different maturities.
- Shapes: Normal (upward-sloping — healthy growth expectations), Inverted (short-term yields exceed long-term — often recession signal), Flat.
- Research use: Yield curve shape informs broader economic cycle positioning (cross-reference Domain 1).

**8. Structured Debt Instruments**
- Types: Securitized debt (pass-through certificates), Perpetual bonds (AT1 bonds for banks — no maturity, coupon skip risk), Convertible debentures.
- Research use: Assess seniority, loss-absorption features, and coupon skip conditions carefully — these instruments carry equity-like risk despite being classified as debt.

## Application Framework
1. Cross-check equity research conclusions on financial risk against credit rating trends and bond market spreads for corroboration.
2. Always review covenant structure and headroom for leveraged companies, not just headline leverage ratios.
3. Track any history of restructuring or near-default events as a standing risk flag, even post-resolution.

## Red Flags / Cautions
- Rating downgrade or negative outlook not yet reflected in equity market pricing.
- Covenant breaches or waivers sought from lenders.
- Reliance on short-term borrowing to fund long-term assets (asset-liability mismatch).
- Complex structured instruments (AT1 bonds, securitization) with opaque risk features.

## Universal Rule Applied
Credit market signals should be treated as verifiable, regulator-disclosed data points (rating agency reports, bond market pricing) — never inferred or assumed without current sourcing.

## Worked Example
A company's equity is still trading near highs, but its NCDs are trading at a yield 250bps wider than 6 months ago, and the rating outlook was revised to "Negative" from "Stable" two months ago. Debt-market signals moved before equity — worth flagging as an early warning even though the stock price hasn't reacted yet.
