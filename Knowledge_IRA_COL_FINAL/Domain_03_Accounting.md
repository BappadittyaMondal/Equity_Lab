# Domain 3 — Accounting
Version: 1.0 | Status: Production Ready

---

## Purpose
Accounting principles determine how economic events are translated into reported numbers. Understanding these principles is essential to interpret financial statements correctly and detect manipulation or aggressive treatment.

---

## Core Concepts

### 1. Revenue Recognition
- Principle: Revenue recognized when performance obligations are satisfied (Ind AS 115 / IFRS 15).
- Methods: Point-in-time (goods delivered) vs. Over-time (long-term contracts, construction).
- Research red flags: Channel stuffing, bill-and-hold arrangements, aggressive percentage-of-completion assumptions.

### 2. Depreciation
- Definition: Systematic allocation of a tangible asset's cost over its useful life.
- Methods: Straight-Line Method (SLM), Written Down Value (WDV)/Reducing Balance.
- Research use: Compare depreciation rate to industry norms; unusually low depreciation can inflate reported profit.

### 3. Amortization
- Definition: Systematic write-off of intangible assets (patents, software, goodwill where applicable) over useful life.
- Research use: Check amortization policy for acquired intangibles — aggressive useful-life assumptions can understate true cost.

### 4. Deferred Tax
- Definition: Tax impact of timing differences between accounting profit and taxable profit.
- Types: Deferred Tax Asset (DTA), Deferred Tax Liability (DTL).
- Research use: Large DTA recognition based on assumed future profits is a judgment-heavy area — assess reasonableness.

### 5. Inventory Accounting
- Methods: FIFO (First-In-First-Out), Weighted Average Cost; LIFO not permitted under Ind AS.
- Valuation: Lower of cost or net realizable value (NRV).
- Research use: Inventory write-downs signal demand weakness or obsolescence; method changes can distort year-on-year comparability.

### 6. Working Capital
- Components: Inventory + Trade Receivables – Trade Payables (Net Working Capital).
- Cash Conversion Cycle = Days Inventory Outstanding + Days Sales Outstanding – Days Payables Outstanding.
- Research use: Rising working capital intensity without corresponding revenue growth signals operational stress or aggressive revenue recognition.

### 7. Capitalization
- Definition: Recording an expenditure as an asset (capex) rather than an expense (opex), spreading cost over future periods.
- Research red flag: Capitalizing routine operating expenses (e.g., regular maintenance, marketing) inflates near-term profit and asset base artificially.

### 8. Provisions
- Definition: Liabilities of uncertain timing or amount, recognized when a present obligation exists and outflow is probable.
- Common types: Provision for doubtful debts, warranty provisions, litigation provisions.
- Research use: Sudden reduction in provisioning levels without business justification can be a profit-management signal.

### 9. Capitalization vs. Exceptional Items
- Definition: Items outside normal recurring operations — asset write-offs, restructuring costs, gains/losses on sale of business.
- Research use: Always separate "core" operating performance from exceptional items to judge sustainable earnings power.

### 10. Accounting Policies
- Definition: Specific principles and methods a company applies in preparing its financial statements, disclosed in notes.
- Research use: Compare policies across peers for like-for-like analysis; flag any mid-year or mid-cycle policy changes and understand the stated rationale.

---

## Application Framework for Research
1. Read the "Significant Accounting Policies" note before analyzing numbers.
2. Compare policy choices (depreciation method, revenue recognition timing) against sector peers.
3. Track any changes in accounting policy year-over-year and quantify their impact on reported profit.
4. Distinguish cash-impacting items from non-cash accounting entries (e.g., DTA/DTL movements).

---

## Red Flags / Cautions
- Frequent changes in useful life assumptions for depreciation/amortization.
- Capitalization of expenses that peers treat as opex.
- Provisions reversed opportunistically to smooth earnings.
- Revenue recognized significantly ahead of cash collection on a sustained basis.

---

## Universal Rule Applied to This Domain
Accounting judgment areas (provisions, useful life, revenue timing) require extra scrutiny — always cross-check against disclosed policy and flag interpretive uncertainty rather than presenting judgment calls as fact.

---

## Worked Example
A company changes depreciation useful life for plant assets from 10 years to 15 years mid-year. Annual depreciation on a ₹150cr asset base falls from ₹15cr to ₹10cr — a ₹5cr non-cash boost directly to PBT with zero underlying operational improvement. Always flag such policy changes and re-compute like-for-like profit for trend comparison.
