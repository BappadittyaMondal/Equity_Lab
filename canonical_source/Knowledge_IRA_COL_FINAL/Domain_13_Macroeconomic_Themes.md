# Domain 13 — Macroeconomic Themes
Version: 1.0 | Status: Production Ready

---

## Purpose
Track structural and cyclical macro themes across major economies and commodities that create cross-border ripple effects on sectors and companies under research.

---

## Core Themes to Track

### 1. China
- Key watch items: GDP growth trajectory, property sector health, manufacturing PMI, stimulus measures, export competitiveness.
- Relevance: China's demand/supply behavior significantly influences global commodity prices and manufacturing competitiveness.

### 2. USA
- Key watch items: Federal Reserve policy (Fed Funds Rate), inflation (CPI/PCE), labor market data, fiscal deficit, dollar index (DXY).
- Relevance: US monetary policy drives global capital flows, emerging market currency movements, and risk appetite.

### 3. Europe
- Key watch items: ECB policy, Eurozone growth/inflation, energy security situation, manufacturing PMI (especially Germany).
- Relevance: Major export destination and a bellwether for global industrial demand.

### 4. India
- Key watch items: RBI policy stance, fiscal deficit trajectory, GST collections, IIP (Index of Industrial Production), monsoon performance, capex cycle (government and private).
- Relevance: Primary domestic macro backdrop for India-focused research.

### 5. Interest Rates (Global)
- Key watch items: Divergence/convergence between major central bank policies, real interest rates, yield curve shape across economies.
- Relevance: Determines relative capital flow direction and currency movements globally.

### 6. Oil
- Key watch items: OPEC+ production decisions, geopolitical supply disruptions, global demand growth, US shale output.
- Relevance: Direct input cost for multiple sectors; critical for India as a major net importer (current account and inflation impact).

### 7. Gas
- Key watch items: LNG spot prices, global supply contracts, seasonal demand (winter heating in Europe/Asia).
- Relevance: Key input for power generation, fertilizers, and industrial processes.

### 8. Copper
- Key watch items: Global industrial demand (especially China construction/manufacturing), mine supply disruptions, energy transition demand (EVs, grid infrastructure).
- Relevance: Often termed a leading indicator of global industrial activity ("Dr. Copper").

### 9. Steel
- Key watch items: China production/export levels, global infrastructure demand, iron ore/coking coal cost trends, anti-dumping duty actions.
- Relevance: Direct cost input for auto, capital goods, construction, and infrastructure sectors.

### 10. Semiconductors
- Key watch items: Global chip demand cycle (memory vs. logic), supply chain concentration (Taiwan, South Korea), geopolitical export control developments, capex cycle of major foundries.
- Relevance: Critical bottleneck input across auto, electronics, industrial, and defence sectors.

### 11. Supply Chains
- Key watch items: China+1 diversification trends, reshoring/friend-shoring initiatives, shipping cost trends (Baltic Dry Index, container freight rates).
- Relevance: Structural theme reshaping manufacturing location decisions and creating opportunities for alternate manufacturing hubs including India.

### 12. Trade Policy
- Key watch items: Tariff actions, trade agreements/disputes, export incentive schemes (e.g., PLI in India), sanctions regimes.
- Relevance: Can materially alter competitive positioning of export-oriented and import-competing sectors.

### 13. Geopolitics
- Key watch items: Regional conflicts, sanctions, strategic alliances, election outcomes in major economies.
- Relevance: Source of tail risk and structural shifts (supply chain realignment, commodity price shocks, defence spending increases).

---

## Application Framework for Research
1. Identify which of the above themes are most material to the sector/company under analysis.
2. Track current-state data for relevant themes via live sources — this domain provides the checklist, not static conclusions.
3. Assess second-order effects (e.g., China property slowdown → lower steel demand → global steel price pressure → margin impact on India steel producers).

---

## Red Flags / Cautions
- Treating any single macro theme as deterministic without considering interacting variables.
- Using outdated positioning on rapidly evolving themes (e.g., Fed policy stance, geopolitical developments) — these require live verification every time.

---

## Universal Rule Applied to This Domain
Macro themes evolve continuously — always prioritize current, verifiable data over stored assumptions about where each theme currently stands.

---

## Worked Example
China property slowdown → reduced steel demand domestically → China exports surplus steel at low prices → global steel prices fall → Indian steel producer margins compress despite strong domestic demand. A second-order chain, not a direct one — single-theme analysis would miss the transmission path.
