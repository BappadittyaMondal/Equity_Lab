# Domain 10 — Portfolio Management
Version: 1.0 | Status: Production Ready

---

## Purpose
Portfolio management principles govern how individual research conclusions translate into a constructed, risk-managed portfolio — connecting single-stock analysis to overall portfolio outcomes.

---

## Core Concepts

### 1. Asset Allocation
- Definition: Distribution of capital across broad asset classes (equity, debt, cash, gold, alternatives).
- Use: The primary determinant of long-term portfolio risk and return characteristics, more impactful than individual security selection.

### 2. Diversification
- Definition: Spreading investments across securities/sectors/asset classes to reduce unsystematic (company/sector-specific) risk.
- Use: Effective diversification requires genuinely uncorrelated holdings, not just a large number of positions.

### 3. Position Sizing
- Definition: Determining how much capital to allocate to each individual holding.
- Approaches: Equal-weight, conviction-weighted, volatility-adjusted (inverse volatility), Kelly Criterion-based sizing.
- Use: Position sizing should reflect both conviction level and risk (business quality, volatility, liquidity).

### 4. Sector Allocation
- Definition: Distribution of portfolio capital across industry sectors.
- Use: Avoid unintended concentration risk; benchmark sector weights against index composition unless deliberately taking active sector bets.

### 5. Risk Budget
- Definition: The total amount of risk (typically measured via volatility or potential drawdown) a portfolio is willing to bear, allocated across positions.
- Use: Ensures no single position or correlated cluster of positions can cause outsized portfolio damage.

### 6. Cash Allocation
- Definition: Proportion of portfolio held in cash or cash-equivalents.
- Use: Provides downside buffer and dry powder for opportunistic deployment; level should reflect market valuation context and risk appetite.

### 7. Correlation
- Definition: Statistical measure of how asset/security returns move together.
- Use: Combining low or negatively correlated assets reduces overall portfolio volatility more effectively than combining high-correlation assets, even at similar expected returns.

### 8. Expected Return
- Definition: Anticipated return of a security or portfolio based on fundamental/valuation analysis and historical context.
- Use: Should be probability-weighted across scenarios (bull/base/bear) rather than a single-point estimate.

### 9. Rebalancing
- Definition: Periodic adjustment of portfolio holdings back to target allocation.
- Approaches: Calendar-based (quarterly/annually), threshold-based (rebalance when allocation drifts beyond a set %).
- Use: Enforces disciplined "sell high, buy low" behavior and controls drift in risk exposure over time.

---

## Application Framework for Research
1. Position-level research (Domains 1-9) feeds into portfolio-level decisions — a great business can still be a poor portfolio fit if it creates excessive concentration or correlation risk.
2. Size positions based on both conviction and risk characteristics, not conviction alone.
3. Regularly review sector/asset allocation against both benchmark and absolute risk tolerance.
4. Maintain a rebalancing discipline to avoid emotionally-driven allocation drift.

---

## Red Flags / Cautions
- Excessive concentration in a single stock, sector, or theme, even if each underlying research thesis is sound.
- Confusing diversification (number of holdings) with true risk reduction (correlation-adjusted).
- Ignoring rebalancing discipline, allowing winners to become oversized risk concentrations by drift.
- Holding zero cash buffer irrespective of valuation extremes.

---

## Universal Rule Applied to This Domain
Portfolio recommendations must be evidence-based and risk-aware — never presented as guaranteed outcomes, and always accompanied by explicit acknowledgment of the assumptions and risk tolerance context involved.

---

## Worked Example
Portfolio holds 8 stocks, all in export-oriented IT/Pharma — looks diversified by count but all move together on rupee/US-demand shocks (correlation ~0.8). True diversification needs a low-correlation addition (e.g., domestic-focused banking/utility), not just a 9th stock in the same basket.
