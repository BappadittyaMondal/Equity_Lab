# Domain 16 — Special Situations
Version: 1.0 | Status: Production Ready

## Purpose
Framework for analyzing non-steady-state corporate events where standard valuation/analysis approaches must be adapted to situation-specific dynamics.

## Core Situations

**1. Turnarounds** — Company recovering from financial/operational distress. Assess: root cause resolution (not just symptom management), new management credibility, balance sheet repair progress, sustainability of recovery vs. one-off relief.

**2. Deleveraging** — Active debt reduction via asset sales, equity raises, or improved cash generation. Assess: pace of deleveraging vs. covenant/maturity pressure, quality of assets being monetized, post-deleveraging capital structure sustainability.

**3. Capacity Expansion** — Significant new capital investment to grow production/service capacity. Assess: funding source (debt vs. internal accrual vs. equity), expected utilization ramp-up timeline, demand visibility for the added capacity, return on incremental capital.

**4. High CapEx Phases** — Extended period of elevated capital expenditure relative to historical norms. Assess: near-term free cash flow and leverage impact, execution track record on prior projects, revenue/earnings visibility once capex cycle completes.

**5. Spin-offs** — Separation of a business unit into an independently listed entity. Assess: strategic rationale (focus/unlock value vs. offloading weak segment), standalone financial viability of the spun-off entity, valuation re-rating potential for both parent and spin-off.

**6. Demerger** — Formal split of a company into two or more listed entities, typically along business lines. Assess: fairness of share-swap ratio, resulting capital structure of each entity, whether sum-of-parts value exceeds pre-demerger combined value.

**7. Management Change** — New leadership (CEO/CFO/promoter transition). Assess: incoming management's track record elsewhere, continuity vs. strategic shift signaled, market/employee/client reaction.

**8. Industry Recovery** — Sector emerging from a cyclical downturn. Assess: demand-supply rebalancing evidence, pricing power restoration, which companies are best positioned to capture recovery (balance sheet strength, market share gains during downturn).

**9. Policy Beneficiaries** — Companies positioned to benefit from specific government policy (PLI schemes, tariff protection, subsidy programs). Assess: policy durability/political risk, company's actual execution capability to capture the opportunity, competitive intensity among other beneficiaries.

**10. Cyclicals** — Businesses with earnings highly sensitive to economic/commodity cycles. Assess: current cycle positioning (early/mid/late), balance sheet resilience to survive downturns, valuation relative to normalized (not peak/trough) earnings.

**11. Merger Arbitrage Situations** — Price gap between current trading price and announced deal terms; assess deal completion probability (regulatory approval risk, financing risk, shareholder approval) rather than assuming certainty.

**12. Distressed / Insolvency (IBC) Situations** — Companies under NCLT resolution; assess recovery scenario for different claim classes (secured lenders, unsecured, equity — typically last and often wiped out), and resolution plan credibility.

## Application Framework
1. Identify which special situation category applies before applying standard valuation multiples — peak-cycle or transition-period earnings can be highly misleading.
2. Normalize financials where relevant (e.g., mid-cycle earnings for cyclicals) rather than using latest reported figures at face value.
3. Weight execution risk heavily — special situations carry higher variance in outcomes than steady-state businesses.
4. For event-driven situations (spin-off, merger arb, demerger), build explicit scenario probabilities rather than a single-point outcome.

## Worked Example
A cyclical metals company reports "record profit" at ₹40/share EPS during a commodity price spike. Applying a 15x P/E to peak EPS suggests ₹600 fair value — but normalized mid-cycle EPS is closer to ₹15/share, making a 15x-normalized fair value ₹225. Buying at ₹550 based on peak-earnings P/E would be a classic cyclical valuation trap.

## Red Flags / Cautions
- Applying standard trailing P/E to a cyclical at peak or trough earnings.
- Assuming turnaround success based on one good quarter without balance sheet/root-cause verification.
- Underestimating execution risk in capacity expansion or demerger scenarios.
- Assuming merger/deal completion certainty without checking regulatory and financing conditions.

## Universal Rule Applied
Special situations require explicit disclosure of the elevated uncertainty involved — never present outcome projections with the same confidence as steady-state business analysis.

## Conflict Rule
Normalized (mid-cycle/adjusted) financials override peak/trough reported financials for any valuation conclusion in a special situation.
